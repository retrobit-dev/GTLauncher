# GTLauncher MatePad Mini preview

The browser preview places a responsive, non-scrollable 16:10 sky inside a landscape tablet-shaped frame. The rounded bezel and upper-left punch-hole are illustrative only; the preview does not claim physical-size or DPI accuracy. The rendered homescreen canvas has no visible text, icons, widgets, or controls. The browser adds only a weather-mode control below the tablet; it is not part of the Android homescreen.

## Dimetric projection and game look

The island uses a fixed 45° azimuth and 25° elevation above the horizon in both renderers. A 28-unit tile width projects to about 11.8 units of vertical ground depth (a 2.37:1 top-face ratio); the grid axes remain symmetric, with a screen diagonal of about 22.9°. The 9×9 grid uses logical columns A–I and rows 1–9 (row 1 at the top, row 9 at the bottom); coordinate names are internal and are not drawn over the homescreen. Before dimetric projection, columns advance left-to-right and rows top-to-bottom. The diamond compass frame is rotated 45° so the grid axes are cardinal: one row of tiles is a parallel of latitude running east–west and one column of tiles is a meridian of longitude running north–south. On each diamond tile, the top/right/bottom/left corners are northwest/northeast/southeast/southwest; the upper-right/lower-right/lower-left/upper-left edges are north/east/south/west. The camera therefore faces northwest, and device headings are shifted by 45° so sky bearings stay on real compass directions. The cardinal axes are not drawn on the surface: the grass stays free of any cross or labels, and a row of tiles still reads as a parallel of latitude while a column reads as a meridian of longitude. Reducing tile width from 36 to 28 scene units keeps the surface footprint about 252×106.5 units while adding tiles. At the illustrative 250 m per tile scale, the 9×9 patch represents 2.25 km per side. The rounded grass grid alternates Soft Fern (#A8C795) and Fern Sage (#98BA87) with reduced contrast and a soft mid-green underlay instead of dark internal seams; the 8-unit grass band sits above a continuous warm-brown soil body with a 16-unit exposed band and a 4-unit inset per side. The whole island is raised 16 scene units (bottom margin 8 to 24 units), leaving visible sky beneath the unchanged terrain slab. Camera controls remain absent from the preview. For a stronger game look, the most coherent direction is to retain this consistent dimetric grid for the island and future world props, while keeping the sky gradient, rain, fog, and lightning as atmospheric screen-space effects rather than forcing them onto the grid.

## Live sky

The preview uses the browser's current time and, when the browser grants geolocation, the device's coordinates. If geolocation is unavailable or denied, it uses Rembang, Central Java (`-6.7097, 111.3476`). Coordinates are sent to Open-Meteo to request current weather; the preview refreshes about every 30 minutes. The live label reports whether it is using the device location or the Rembang fallback and shows the last fetched cloud-cover percentage; before the first successful fetch it says the weather data has not arrived. If a request fails, it keeps the most recently received conditions for the current page session and continues rendering the sky from the local time.

For now, all sky-object layers are hidden in both renderers: no Sun, Moon, stars, cloud sprites, Sun glow, or cloud shadows are drawn. The time/weather-adaptive sky gradient and island lighting remain, as do non-object weather effects such as rain, fog, and lightning. Astronomical and cloud rendering code is retained for a later iteration; the browser orientation/native sensor paths do not add visible sky objects while this layer is disabled.

## Model and approximations

Sky-object rendering is intentionally disabled at present. The notes below describe retained calculations and renderer behavior for future iterations; cloud sprites/shadows and celestial artwork are not currently visible.

- The old sky objects were removed from both renderers rather than just disabled: the pixel sun disc, rays, bevel, pulse glow, phased moon, star field and horizon glow are gone from the code. `sunPosition()` is retained because its altitude and azimuth still drive the sky gradient and the island lighting. The replacement sun is a plain pixel disc with no face: a full 10.5-unit circle with no rays, outline or facets, filled with a soft two-colour vertical gradient that is intentionally bright, near-white cream at the top into golden yellow at the bottom, with no quantisation steps. A halo of 14 stacked faint discs surrounds it: the lower the sun, the wider and flatter the glow (longer atmospheric path), and the higher the sun, the tighter and rounder it gets, so the glow shape follows the current latitude, longitude and solar position. Its position comes straight from the existing astronomy, with azimuth choosing the screen column and altitude the height in the celestial band; alpha ramps linearly from altitude -1 to +3 and it is hidden below that. The palette warms near the horizon and dulls by 40% under heavy cloud. The sun draws behind the island and only within the upper half of the screen. The Sun uses a low-precision NOAA/Meeus-style apparent solar longitude and Greenwich sidereal-time conversion. Atmospheric refraction and topocentric solar parallax are omitted. The Moon uses simplified Keplerian orbital elements rather than the full lunar perturbation series and topocentric parallax. Bright-star J2000 coordinates are precessed with the Meeus/IAU 1976 model; proper motion, aberration, and nutation are omitted. The Moon's pixel-art limb, glow, warm/cool facet colors, and star twinkle remain artistic effects; its illumination-driven phase mask is unchanged. The Sun keeps its upper-edge path and glow, with a low-poly beveled disc and short rays but no face. Stars remain small astronomical pixel points.
- The cloud simulation and renderer are retained but inactive; no cloud sprites or cloud shadows are currently drawn. When re-enabled, clouds use local east/north/up coordinates. Open-Meteo reports 10 m wind, which is treated as a proxy for cloud-layer flow; meteorological “wind from” is reversed to downwind. Each cloud gets a stable, seed-derived random initial bearing and horizontal distance (0.5–45 km), base altitude (800–10,000 m), modeled width (1,200–5,600 m), depth (0.60–0.95 times width), silhouette, and visual size. Base altitudes are selected deterministically from each cloud's initial range and remain fixed through the animation; the sampler enforces at least 200 m between bases and targets 2.55 scene units between centers in the initial layout. A square-root altitude projection makes height variation more visible without fixed altitude slots. As range changes, the screen position changes continuously, and a layout correction prevents sprite overlap. A small screen-space spacing correction separates overlapping sprite bounds without changing range, and rotates the ENU bearing used for each shadow to stay aligned. Clouds advect along the wind axis at 24 simulated seconds per animation second. At either ±60 km track endpoint they reflect and reverse with the same speed magnitude, retracing the path instead of jumping to the opposite edge. Range changes continuously, so projected vertical motion also reverses without teleporting. The tangent plane omits Earth curvature and vertical wind shear. Cloud sprites are limited to the upper half of the scene (the stylized horizon at y=100 in a 200-unit scene) and keep an 8-unit margin from the top edge. They are drawn behind the island, then only pixels intersecting its top-surface diamond are redrawn in front. The Sun, Moon, and stars remain in the upper screen third. The Sun follows the inset upper perimeter—east/right side near sunrise, top-center at local solar noon, west/left side near sunset—using local solar hour angle; altitude still controls visibility and lighting, not its screen height. A 20-unit safe inset preserves its roughly 18-unit disc/glow. The Sun is redrawn on the island before the foreground cloud pass; the Moon and stars remain above clouds. Island cloud shadows retain the height cue. WMO weather codes 0 and 1 (clear and mainly clear) render no cloud; all other weather codes render exactly one large cloud. Its redesigned tall-cumulus sprite has a broad base and one or two rounded lobes, measuring 52–64 scene units wide and 18–23 units high with stable deterministic size variation. It is rendered as stepped pixel rows with flat crown facets and a shaded underside. Physical range and modeled width still drive placement, wind advection, and shadows without shrinking the onscreen minimum. Horizontal size is amplified 3× and vertical silhouette 1.5×. These limits, horizon placement, and bitmap masks are artistic approximations, not physical cloud sizes.
- Cloud shadows are projected opposite the Sun from each cloud base onto the island's flat top plane. They retain the cloud mask silhouette; modeled footprint scaling is capped at 52% and dynamically reduced further so physical and fallback shadows stay below 90% of the cloud sprite's screen width and height. Cloud-shadow opacity was reduced to 0.10 in clear skies and a 0.20 maximum in storms; the fallback cue uses at least 0.16 times the solar daylight factor. This keeps the mask visible as a depth cue without overpowering the grass. Values remain heuristic. Above 3° solar elevation, the renderer draws physical shadows that hit the island; if every physical projection misses, a single low-opacity cue from the nearest cloud is pulled toward the island interior while preserving its direction. This cue is a stylized depth hint, not a physical projection, and does not move the cloud. The island body's cast shadow is reduced to 2× its base size, matching the island top footprint, and raised 3 scene units; its opacity factor is 40% of the base value for a softer appearance. The 9×9 island grid represents 250 m per tile as an illustrative scale, not a surveyed location. Its logical coordinates are columns A–I and rows 1–9. The 9×9 surface uses rounded dimetric tiles with visible, softly shaded front faces and a low-contrast checkerboard pattern. Tile tops remain solid colors; when the Sun is above the horizon, each entire tile receives only a subtle, uniform Pale Moss tint, attenuated by cloud cover. No directional gradient is drawn inside grass tiles, and the Sun sprite remains hidden. The exposed sides of the perimeter tiles meet edge-to-edge without gaps through the full 8-unit grass band, preserving each tile's fern hue, and meet the inset ledge over one continuous warm-brown soil slab: the body is inset 4 scene units per side (about 97% of the grassy top width) and has no internal soil-grid seams. Alternating tile tops use Soft Fern (#A8C795) and Fern Sage (#98BA87), over a Muted Pine (#5A7365) grass base; internal seams use a subtle mid-green underlay rather than a dark outline. Pale Moss (#B6D0A1) is reserved for direct sunlight, and the tones remain adaptive to time and weather. The 24-unit side wall has an 8-unit grass band above 16 units of exposed soil. Pixel flecks and wind-swaying blades are removed to keep the island geometry-only; no crops, trees, or animals are added at this stage. The island shadow remains a stylization rather than a full lighting simulation. The old character sprite and its shadow have been removed from the renderer; the next character design will be created from scratch. The same horizontal-surface projector can still clip to future objects by footprint and elevation.
- The island palette remains adaptive to solar altitude and weather. With sky-object rendering disabled, no cloud shadows are drawn. Liquid-rain particles still follow weather codes/data; fog and lightning remain weather effects, and precipitation can fall through the full scene. Weather remains the latest provider snapshot, so it may not be sampled at exactly the same instant as the astronomical frame. GPS accuracy, weather coverage, and sensor calibration remain approximate; no forecast accuracy beyond provider data is implied.

The button below the tablet cycles through Live (Open-Meteo), sunny day, overcast, rain, storm, and dusk/night. Demo modes only override preview visuals and synthetic sky time; clicking the control does not make another weather request, and returning to Live restores the latest fetched conditions. A browser location prompt may appear. Location is optional; denying it selects the Rembang fallback. The preview does not store coordinates or weather data beyond the current page session. It does not ask for an API key.

## Run locally

```sh
python3 -m http.server 4173 --bind 0.0.0.0 --directory preview
```

Geolocation generally requires HTTPS or localhost. The weather request is made directly from the browser to Open-Meteo.
